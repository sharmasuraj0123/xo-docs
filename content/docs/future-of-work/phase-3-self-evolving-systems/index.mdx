## What it is

<Lead>The amount of intent it takes to make a real artefact keeps collapsing.</Lead>

<Flow
	steps={[
		{ icon: "users", title: "A team", desc: "What a project once took." },
		{ icon: "clock", title: "An afternoon", desc: "Then one person, a few hours." },
		{ icon: "lightning", title: "An hour", desc: "Then a single sitting." },
		{ icon: "chat", title: "A sentence", desc: "Soon, for many things, just intent." },
	]}
/>

When agents can act on that shrinking intent, software stops being something you
ship once and maintain by hand. It becomes something that adapts continuously:
agents write and revise their own skills, refactor and deploy code, watch
production, and respond to what they see.

## Autonomous agency

<Lead>A fleet maintains and extends the systems it runs on, instead of waiting for a release cycle.</Lead>

<Cycle />

This is the far end of the arc that runs single agent, to org, to autonomous
agency. The organization's software and the workforce that runs it stop being
separate things. The system evolves with the work, proposing changes, testing
them, and improving the workspace itself over time.

## What stays human

<Lead>Self-evolving does not mean unsupervised.</Lead>

As execution gets cheaper, judgment and accountability get more valuable, not
less. The open problem is institutional: apprenticeship, trust, and verification
have to keep pace with capability, or we get systems that change fast and
confidently in the wrong direction.

If XO is only a launcher, this phase is dangerous. If XO is the place where agent
work is supervised, bounded, recovered, audited, and handed off, this phase
becomes labor infrastructure rather than a leap of faith.

## Honest uncertainty

This is the most speculative phase, and the right posture is humility. It is
reachable from where we are, but not guaranteed, and the deciding factor is less
the capability than whether we build the institutions for trust and verification
alongside it. Anyone who tells you confidently how this lands is either wrong or
selling something.
